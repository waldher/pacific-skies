The yellow bearing and named course direct me to the outpost. MAP opens the chart.
- **Integrated status.** HULL 27% appears beside the plane in the second set across all four matched urgency captures. Its placement next to the bracket holds the association on the carrier bow even when wings and deck lines intersect. The dark tag behind it helps on fallback and short landscape. It is tight against an overlapping aircraft on the bow, especially in portrait. The upper-left hull value remains easy to confirm. Heat is still a distinct, remote readout.
- **Contextual guidance.** The urgency panel drops the nearest-base secondary line, allowing the named outpost to stand alone. The course panel sits more clearly apart from the hull panel in short landscape. The local course cue is less prominent in the healthy stills (28 and 40), which cleans up the runway but makes the direction slightly harder to see against grass and markings. Main objective and minimap remain legible. In a damage state the removed nearest-base ETA could be useful if the current objective is still attack.
- **Phone legibility and action.** Map and ordnance remain visibly separate. The capture report records opening the chart at all four tested sizes. The hot fire control still says FIRE / HOLD RIGHT, despite looking unavailable. That leaves an urgent non-color readability blocker in the tested heat condition. A visually full bar alone cannot tell the player whether fire has locked out or is close to doing so. Actual touch hit areas, two-thumb use, and physical safe areas remain unverified.
- **Character and restraint.** The urgency callout adds useful text only in the tested low hull condition. The interface leaves wing and nose detail free, though the extra callout shares the congested bow with nearby aircraft. Healthy stills retain the period instrument treatment and the compact panel arrangement.

## Publisher references

- **Dead Space integration — falls short.** Isaac's segmented strip is visibly part of his suit. The new low-hull tag gives a direct read of the controlled aircraft, but it remains a detached UI label and heat still requires looking away.
- **Ghost of Tsushima environmental guidance and restraint — falls short.** The exploration still leaves the terrain and shrine open, and the wind breakdown shows particles carrying the destination bearing through the landscape. The new HUD reduces competing signals but still relies on a directional symbol and an always-visible course panel. In combat, the reference leaves most of the actor and arena open. In Pacific Skies a busy carrier can still bring aircraft behind the course panel.
