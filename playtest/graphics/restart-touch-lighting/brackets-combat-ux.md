# Pilot brackets combat HUD review

Independent comparison of the six pilot-bracket stills with the corresponding
chevron and ring stills. All are 852×393, with coast, dogfight, and stress
captured at the native phone and fallback quality settings. This is a visual
inspection at presentation size. It does not measure response time, live
tracking, thumb reach, control semantics, or continuous motion.

- **Coast, both qualities.** The pale open brackets are the only paired marker
  and clearly frame the aircraft. They are easier to associate with the pilot
  than the low chevron, which resembles the nearby navigation arrows. Aircraft
  markings remain unobstructed, unlike the filled ring.
- **Dogfight, both qualities.** The paired marks sit across the pilot's
  horizontal axis and draw the eye to the aircraft cluster at the bow. Their
  center falls on the aircraft rather than below the ship. The gold aim circle
  is still nearby, but the shape and color differ. The bracket pair reads more
  immediately than the chevron and avoids the ring's tinted overlay.
- **Stress, both qualities.** I could pick out the bracketed pilot position on
  first inspection, including in the softened fallback scene. Enemy planes,
  bursts, and the deck can still overlap the pilot model, so the marker does
  more to establish its position than to separate every wing or identify its
  aircraft type. The wide left-right span includes nearby clutter, particularly
  at the bow. It does not look like a cue beneath or pointing to the ship.
- **Status and controls.** Map, objective, hull, heat, ordnance and Fire are
  as visible as in the prior captures. Small status text and the similar
  weight of the objective and nearest-base rows remain existing concerns.

**Verdict for this slice: pass.** The brackets recover the chevron's loss of
pilot-position findability without reintroducing the overlay. Carry the trial
to continuous playback and the remaining HUD tasks. In moving combat, check
that the brackets stay paired and near the aircraft through banking, effects
and screen-edge transitions. This limited result is not a full HUD verdict.
