# Vought F4U-1 Corsair — reference dimensions

Reference data behind the `CORSAIR` spec table in `tools/build-aircraft.mjs`.
Everything is in metres. Fuselage stations (`x`) are measured aft from the
propeller spinner tip, heights from the thrust line, spans from the
centreline. The generator's origin is the mid-point of the overall length,
so model `z = x − 5.08`.

## Published figures

| Item | Value | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Wingspan | 12.50 m (41 ft 0 in) | Sets the game scale (`CONFIG.render.aircraftWingspan`) |
| Length | 10.16 m (33 ft 4 in) | F4U-1/-1A/-1D; the F4U-4 is 33 ft 8 in |
| Height | 4.90 m (16 ft 1 in) | Tail down on its gear; not modelled |
| Wing area | 29.17 m² (314 sq ft) | Model planform ≈ 28.7 m² |
| Propeller | Hamilton Standard Hydromatic, 3 blades, 4.06 m (13 ft 4 in) | Largest fighter prop of its day; the reason for the gull wing |
| Cockpit | Moved 0.81 m (32 in) aft from the prototype | Windscreen sits just behind the wing's mid-chord |

Sources: Wikipedia, *Vought F4U Corsair* (design section and F4U-4
specification table); Skytamer F4U-1D page (span, length, height, area).
The USRA scale-racing minimum-dimension sheet (`usrainfo.org/f4u.pdf`)
was a useful proportion cross-check: at a 100 in span it lists a 39.25 in
stabiliser span (→ 4.9 m), 20 in / 14 in wing root / tip chords, an 11 in
cowl (→ 1.38 m) and a 76 in fuselage.

## Paint scheme (as modelled)

The game is set in 1942, and the Corsair's first combat (VMF-124,
February 1943) was flown in the scheme the Navy had used since
20 August 1941: non-specular **Blue Gray** (M-485, nearest FS 35189) on
all upper surfaces and non-specular **Light Gray** (M-495, nearest
FS 36440) below, with the demarcation low on the fuselage sides. The
tri-colour Sea Blue / Intermediate Blue / White scheme arrived in early
1943 and the overall Glossy Sea Blue the old GLB wore is a 1944 finish.

| Element | Colour | Model value |
|---|---|---|
| Upper surfaces, cowl, canopy frames, fin | Blue Gray ≈ FS 35189 | `#556878` |
| Undersides | Light Gray ≈ FS 36440 | `#bcbfb8` |
| National insignia | Insignia Blue (FS 35044) disc and outline, Insignia White star and bars | `#1c2b48`, `#f0eee6` |
| Walkway | black non-slip on the starboard wing root | `#1f2225` |
| Propeller | black blades, 4 in yellow tips, natural-metal hub | `#161d24`, `#e0b03a` |

Insignia: built to the AN-I-9b proportions rather than eyeballed. With
disc radius R, the star is inscribed in the disc (true pentagram, inner
radius 0.382 R), the white bars are R long and R/2 tall on each side, and
a blue outline R/8 wide surrounds disc and bars. That is the August 1943
form and the one most people picture on a Corsair, so the generator uses
it by default. Strictly, 1942 aircraft carried the plain white star on a
blue disc (red centre dropped May 1942, bars added June 1943, red outline
June–August 1943); set `insignia.bars: false` in the spec for that. The
model carries the insignia on both upper wings for readability; from
February 1943 the rule was upper-left wing and lower-right wing only.
Squadron numbers, tail codes and the fuselage insignia are omitted at
this scale.

Sources: Tailhook Topics, *A brief, F4U Corsair-oriented history of Navy
color schemes and markings* (tailspintopics.blogspot.com, June 2011);
theworldwars.net *Aircraft colors and camouflage, US Navy*.
